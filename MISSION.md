# Mission: Big Tech SWE Interview Prep (Google, AWS, Microsoft)

## Why
Austin is an experienced software engineer who is rusty on interview-specific skills and expects to interview for SWE roles at Google, AWS, and Microsoft within the next ~8-12 weeks (target window: by early-mid November 2026). He needs to rebuild DSA fluency, refresh system design, and prep behavioral/Leadership-Principles rounds — while understanding how these interviews have actually changed since LLMs and AI coding assistants became widespread, so prep time isn't wasted on an outdated interview model.

## Success looks like
- Solve medium-difficulty LeetCode-style problems in Golang within a 25-35 minute window, narrating approach out loud (interview format).
- Recognize and correctly apply the core algorithm patterns (two pointers, sliding window, BFS/DFS, DP, graphs, heaps, tries, etc.) to novel problems, not just memorized ones.
- Run a structured system design interview (requirements → high-level design → deep dive → trade-offs) for a medium-scope system in ~45 minutes.
- Deliver STAR-format answers mapped to AWS's 16 Leadership Principles, and equivalent Google/Microsoft behavioral competencies.
- Correctly describe current (2025-2026) interview-day realities at each company: what's changed post-LLM (AI-assistant policies, format shifts, take-home changes), so expectations match reality on interview day.

## Constraints
- ~8-12 week timeline (started 2026-08-19) — pacing should assume steady but not full-time study.
- Rusty-but-experienced: skip CS-101 explanations of what an array/loop is; focus on interview-specific patterns and rebuilding speed/fluency.
- All coding practice in Golang.
- Explicitly wants the "post-LLM" current interview landscape covered as its own thread, not just classic DSA/system-design material.
- Targeting general SWE (not a specified level/track yet — revisit if a specific team/level emerges).

## Out of scope
- Companies beyond Google, AWS, Microsoft (revisit if the search expands).
- Non-SWE interview tracks (e.g., EM, PM, data science).
- Languages other than Go for coding practice.
